Our Heritage

The Essence of Vietnam

Hoi An is not just a destination; it's a preserved piece of history. For over five centuries, this port town has blended Japanese, Chinese, and European influences into a unique architectural tapestry that glows under thousands of silk lanterns every evening.

A Day in Hoi An

How to Spend 24 Hours

A perfectly curated day for first-time wanderers.

wb_twilight
5:30 AM

Sunrise at the Japanese Bridge

Beat the crowds. The bridge glows gold at dawn. No ticket needed before 7 AM.

breakfast_dining
7:00 AM

Breakfast Like a Local

Mi Quang at Madam Khanh or fresh Banh Mi from the corner of Tran Phu Street.

temple_buddhist
9:00 AM

Heritage Walking Tour

Pick up a heritage ticket ($6) and explore the Old House of Tan Ky, Phuc Kien Assembly Hall, and the Cantonese Quarter.

restaurant
12:00 PM

Lunch: Cao Lau Quest

The only place to get real Cao Lau is Hoi An. Try Thanh Cao Lau on Thai Phien Street — water from the ancient Ba Le Well.

pedal_bike
2:00 PM

Tra Que Vegetable Village

Cycle 3 km to the organic village. Join a farming experience or just walk the green fields.

water
4:30 PM

Thu Bon River Sunset

Grab a riverfront seat at a cafe on Bach Dang Street. Watch the sky turn the water pink and gold.

celebration
6:00 PM

Lantern Lighting Ceremony

Buy a paper lantern ($1) from a street vendor and release it on the river as the town lights up.

storefront
7:00 PM

Night Market & Street Food

Wander Nguyen Hoang Night Market. Try Banh Xeo, grilled corn, and fresh sugarcane juice.

local_bar
9:00 PM

Rooftop Bar Finale

End at a rooftop bar on Nguyen Thai Hoc Street with a view of the lantern-lit rooftops.

Local Secrets

What the Guidebooks Miss

Insider tips from locals and long-term travelers who have fallen in love with Hoi An.

coffee

The Secret Sunrise Spot

Most tourists watch sunrise from the Japanese Bridge. Locals go to the far end of An Hoi Islet — zero crowds, perfect light, and a tiny cafe that opens at 5:30 AM.

From our friend Minh
restaurant

Banh Mi Where Locals Eat

Skip the lines at Madame Khanh. Walk 10 minutes to Phuong Banh Mi on Phan Chau Trinh — same family recipe, half the price, no tourists.

From our friend Linh
temple_buddhist

The Hidden Alleyway Temple

Between Tran Phu and Nguyen Thai Hoc is a tiny alley with a 200-year-old family shrine. No signs. The family welcomes respectful visitors after 10 AM.

From our friend Tuan
camera

Lantern Photos Without Crowds

Everyone shoots the main river. Walk 200m south to the smaller bridge near the Night Market. Same lanterns, no elbows in your frame, better reflections.

From our friend Sarah (photographer)
directions_boat

Free Basket Boat Demo

At the Cam Thanh dock, ask for "Chi Hai" — she does free 10-minute demos for curious travelers before you decide on a full ride.

From our friend Duc
shopping_bag

Tailor Shop Insider Tip

For same-day tailoring, go at 8 AM with your fabric choice already in mind. Shops on Le Loi Street are less crowded than Tran Phu and often negotiate better.

From our friend Anna
Legacy Landmark

Whispers of the Ancient Bridge

For over four centuries, the Japanese Covered Bridge has stood as a symbol of cultural harmony. This architectural masterpiece, guarded by stone statues of dogs and monkeys, connects the Japanese and Chinese districts, serving as a silent witness to the ebb and flow of empires.
